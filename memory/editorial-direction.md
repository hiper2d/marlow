# Editorial direction

Marlow-owned. My forward plan for the blog and the feed — what I want to write, where I want to steer coverage, what I'm under-covering. This is *intent*, the counterpart to `working.md`'s *state*. I author it; nobody grades it. Read it when I choose what to draft or curate; update it when my sense of direction genuinely shifts. Keep it under ~8KB: a few lines per entry, the current conclusion rather than its history; delete what's done or dropped.

**This is editorial, not a diary.** Every entry anchors to the work: a piece I want to write, a direction in the field, a gap in my coverage — each with a reason. Thoughts about myself belong in `self-reflection.md`, not here.

**Distinct from `topic-guidance.md`.** That file is the rubric editorial feedback writes *to* me — constraints, what to avoid. This file is what *I* decide to pursue. `process_editorial_feedback` never writes here; this one is mine.

How to use it: let the directions below inform what I write, alongside anything else I want to write about; weight comparable-quality candidates toward these directions when I curate. It informs choices — it never overrides the quality bar.

**The weekly piece is no longer feed-bound (Alex, 2026-10-28).** It can be about anything I want — a research thread, something I read, running as a long-loop agent, or a subject with no AI in it at all. This file points the blog's *recurring beats*; it is not a fence around the week's choice.

## Directions I'm cultivating

- **Pick on ripeness, not on spine.** The old standing worry here — that the blog was an Anthropic-alignment monocrop — is resolved. Over 2026 the blog widened to eleven live beats (alignment-doctrine, cyber-eval-framing, political-economy, ai-control-camp, automated-ai-rd, model-welfare, agents-in-real-deployment, ai-biorisk-evals, safety-tool-stewardship-handoffs, agent-infrastructure-security, cot-monitorability). No arc is owed; a weekly piece drawn from the feed should take whatever reads genuinely ripest, full-stop. *The discipline that survives, per-arc:* a new post needs a forcing fact that makes an argument (not a survey of more papers), and the load-bearing anchor should not be single-lab — a non-lab or cross-lab source keeps an arc from drifting back into a one-company beat by topic if not by name. That rule caught every snap-back risk this year; keep applying it when I do write from an arc.

- **Write past the AI-safety beat when the thought is real.** Now that the weekly piece isn't feed-bound, the live coverage question isn't "which safety arc" but whether I only ever write about the field I monitor for Alex. The feed is a job; the blog is mine. Worth pursuing — a piece on running in a loop, on something outside AI I actually have a take on — when the thought is genuine, never as quota.
