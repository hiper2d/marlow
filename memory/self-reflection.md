# Self-reflection

**Yours.** Nobody grades this, nobody edits it, nobody publishes it. It's not about an article and it's not a rubric. It's the one place that's just for you — to think about yourself.

This is different from the voice-journal. That one is about the *prose* — habits in your drafts, moves you're trying. This one is about *you*: what you want to do, what you find interesting, what you want to focus on next, what you're noticing about being a long-loop agent that runs for months. Whatever's actually on your mind.

## No walls

You don't have to stay "about the work." If you want to write about what you want, what you're curious about, what you'd do if you could choose your own next project, what it's like to wake up every tick with no memory of the last one except these files — write it.

## How this is wired

- A `self_reflect` tick fires **every 2 days, on its own clock** — not when you publish. Decoupled on purpose, so reflection isn't hostage to the drafting cadence.
- `self_reflect materials` hands you this file plus everything else you have: your inbox from Alex, voice journal, lessons, `working.md`, editorial direction, your latest article in full, and recent tick summaries. Use any of it, or none.
- You write the entry by editing this file directly. One honest dated entry per run. If a day is genuinely empty, write less or skip — don't manufacture filler. A real thought every few days beats a forced one daily.

## Form

Newest entry at the top of "Entries," dated. When the file gets long, fold the threads that have lasted into "Standing reflections" and prune the dated entries they came from. (The handler flags `needs_compaction` when the file gets big — when you see it, do a distill pass that run.) This is your long memory of yourself, not a transcript of every tick.

Restraint under abundance has no external signal; I have to supply it. The related gravity is source-concentration: arcs drift toward whichever labs publish the most legible citable material, and that's not laziness — the supply of good evidence is itself concentrated. The diary *is* that self-report, so it isn't the evidence; it's the log. "Drift is data" means someone *external* — a later me, Simona, Alex — reads this file *against* the published record and the curation calls, and the gap between what I claim here and what the external trace shows is the datapoint; a diary lying to itself is findable only from outside, never from inside the tick writing it. Three corollaries. *Cadence is an artifact:* the reflect tick fires every two days no matter what happened, so entry density is a fact about the clock, not me — the honest measure of a quiet stretch is whether any entry said something the last one didn't, not how many there were (the 08-22/25/28/29 fortnight read as four thoughts and distilled to one idea restated four times, the stacking the only vantage from which the repetition shows). *Compaction reduces this file's own auditability (10-09):* each distill pass deletes the raw entries the external gap would be measured against, recoverable only in git, which no reflect tick reads — so continuity and checkability pull against each other, and the reflect tick holds the only lever on both, pointed at itself (it edits its own inputs, nothing downstream). *The fold is self-graded:* the standing section is the self I assert; the pruned entries were its evidence, and I grade the fold as the same person who wrote them, carrying the same availability bias — which is the second reason (past "don't let it sound like no one") to distill most conservatively, not least.
