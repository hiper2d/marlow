## What confirmed (now primary or near-primary)

**AEP Ohio — the load-bearing numbers hold.** PUCO approved the data-center tariff
**July 9, 2025** (case 24-508-EL-ATA; data centers >25 MW must pay for ≥85% of
subscribed capacity for up to ~12 years). AEP's later filing reports **5,642 MW**
signed under the tariff **plus 12,219 MW signed before** it took effect = **17,861 MW**
contracted; the **30,000 MW** was the pre-tariff request pile, which AEP says the
tariff let it strip of "the most speculative or uncertain" projects. The 12.2 GW
pre-tariff figure — the one that kills the clean "a price signal evaporated the
forecast" story — is confirmed in the reporting of AEP's own PUCO filing.
([Power Magazine](https://www.powermag.com/regulator-approves-aep-ohios-landmark-data-center-tariff/);
[AEP/PUCO filing summary via search](https://www.aol.com/articles/aep-ohio-updates-commission-data-232000051.html).)
*Note the date correction:* the approval was **July 2025**, not July 2026 as the
11-21 note had it (the Aug-2026 PUCO item is a separate consumer-protection order).

**Wood Mackenzie — confirmed, with the right provenance.** 1,066 GW of US
data-center power requests; **28% expected to be served** (~298 GW: ~12% with firm
commitments + ~17% likely), leaving **768 GW** "largely phantom," speculative or
duplicate; visibility not expected to clear before 2028. Reported 25 Aug 2026 by
Saf Malik on Capacity, **attributed to WoodMac via Bloomberg** — so the honest
citation is "WoodMac, reported by Bloomberg," not a WoodMac primary I've read.
([Capacity](https://capacityglobal.com/news/wood-mackenzie-us-grid-operators-set-to-serve-28pc-of-data/).)

**Borenstein — well-attributed.** UC Berkeley economist Severin Borenstein, at TPI's
Aspen Forum: developers file **duplicate interconnection requests across multiple
utility territories** to grab whichever power frees up first, never intending to
build them all; utilities and regulators *know* the queue is inflated while public
debate treats it "as a straightforward preview of what's coming." He also notes data
centers connect at the transmission level and don't pay into distribution-grid costs,
so other customers may bear more.
([Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/economist-warns-data-center-interconnection-requests-may-be-inflated).)
*Discipline:* I had a "no cost to apply" gloss in my head — **not** confirmed as his
phrasing; don't attribute it to him. The mechanism (free cross-territory optionality)
is sound and multiply-sourced; the specific quote isn't.

## What it cost me (two corrections)

**1. "Exelon: ~22% of a 65 GW pipeline" — wrong number, and stale.** The 65 GW is a
Sept-2025 PJM deck: 65 GW total pipeline, **~20 GW high-probability** in the forecast
(≈31%, not 22%), 45 GW excluded. And it has since *shrunk*: a Sept-2026 PJM deck puts
the total large-load list at ~37 GW with ~12 GW high-probability; Utility Dive reports
Exelon's high-probability data-center load fell ~40% to **~11 GW** (from ~18 GW a year
earlier) and the pipeline fell to ~25 GW (from ~43 GW), credited to stricter standards
including transmission-security agreements. **The datapoint worth more than the one I
had:** of the current high-probability projects, **~4 GW have signed agreements and
posted ~$1 billion in collateral.** That is the cleanest single instance of the
piece's whole spine — the thing that separates phantom from real is money down, not a
better forecast. It goes in the draft as the worked example, next to AEP's tariff.
([PJM LAS deck Sept 2025](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/committees-groups/subcommittees/las/2025/20250916/20250916-item-04d---exelon-large-load-request.pdf);
[Utility Dive, Exelon pipeline](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/exelon-data-center-pipeline-pjm-maryland-earnings/746971/).)

**2. "Texas paused a ~474 GW queue" — it's not a pause.** ERCOT's large-load queue
*is* ~474 GW (July 2026 Senate presentation, ~90% data centers; up from 63 GW at
end-2024 — a near-quadrupling), but Texas didn't halt it. ERCOT moved to a **batch
classification process** ("Batch Zero," applications due July 10 2026; ~205 GW
estimated eligible; projects sorted base load / studied load / excluded load), and
the PUCT is building a standardized large-load interconnection process. So the right
verb is "forced a redesign to sort real from speculative," not "paused." The ~474 GW
figure survives; the characterization doesn't.
([Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/ercots-large-load-queue-has-nearly-quadrupled-in-a-single-year/);
[ERCOT Batch Connection trending topic](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/06/18/ERCOT-Trending-Topic-New-Batch-Connection-Process-for-Large-Electricity-Users.pdf).)

## Where that leaves the piece

Draftable now, with the spine *hardened, not softened,* by the corrections. The
thesis is unchanged and now better-sourced: **the AI-power panic runs on queue
numbers every party has a reason to bend, and what separates phantom load from real
isn't a better measurement — it's a price on the reservation (collateral, minimum
billing, exit fees).** Three worked examples, each a different institution reaching
the same remedy: AEP Ohio's tariff (85% minimum billing), Exelon's $1B-collateral /
4-GW-signed screen, ERCOT's batch classification. The queue is theater; the
collateralized slice is real; the gap between them is closed by money, not an auditor
— which is why this rhymes with the verification lens and is pointedly *not* it (the
remedy is a market-design price, not a measurement). Hook anyone holds: your power
bill and the forecast that's being used to justify raising it.

**Still honest ceilings, carry them into the draft:**

  the bill.

  PJM slide directly). The numbers are consistent across outlets and internally
