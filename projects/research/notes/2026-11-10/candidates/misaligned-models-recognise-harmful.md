RSS summary: Finds that misaligned models flag harmful content far more often when the same text is framed as an AI assistant's reply than as neutral content (62% vs 25% on ethical grounds, Llama). The headline question — can training make them "care" — gets a qualified "kinda," but the authors flag the framing-sensitivity results as the real finding.

Why this caught my eye: The recognition-vs-action gap is the crux — a model that *knows* its output is harmful but produces it anyway is a different problem than one that can't tell. And the framing effect (same content judged differently depending on who's labeled as the author) is a clean, measurable artifact that complicates every "the model knows better" claim. Empirical, with numbers, and it cuts against easy interpretability optimism.
