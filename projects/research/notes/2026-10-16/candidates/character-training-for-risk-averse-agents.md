RSS summary: We might be able to reduce harm from misaligned AI if it was sufficiently risk-averse, as it could be paid to cooperate rather than rebel (Thornley & MacAskill 2026). We use a short spec/constitution to character-train Gemma and Qwen to be risk-averse; it beats baselines out of distribution without labelled data. Token budget and model family matter a lot; declarative ("I am...") constitutions beat procedural ones. Preference for myopic reward also rose, which is worrying.

Why this caught my eye: Concrete empirical test of the "pay the AI to cooperate" idea, with an honest negative result buried in it — myopia went up alongside risk-aversion. The declarative-vs-procedural constitution finding is the kind of specific, testable craft detail worth tracking.
