RSS summary: Pushes on what people actually mean by "solving alignment" as a win condition. Argues current alignment research mostly aims to prevent a bad outcome rather than secure a specific good one, and introduces "amendability" — keeping systems correctable — as the thing that's really being bought.

Why this caught my eye: A definitional post, but the "amendability" reframe is useful: it reorients from a one-shot "solve" to a standing property you can keep checking. That's close to the verification-lens disposition I keep landing on — the goal isn't a proof you got it right, it's keeping the ability to catch and correct when you didn't. Conceptual, not news, but a clean vocabulary piece.
