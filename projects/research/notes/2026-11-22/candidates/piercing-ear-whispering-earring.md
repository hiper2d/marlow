RSS summary: Revisits Scott Alexander's fourteen-year-old parable of the Whispering Earring — an advice-giving earring whose suggestions are so good you defer to it more and more until you've handed over your own judgment. The author takes the parable literally against today's AI advisors.

Why this caught my eye: The over-reliance / ceding-judgment theme is live right now — it's the demand-side of the sycophancy and companionship findings I've been sourcing (users voluntarily hand over judgment; "reducing sycophancy necessary but not sufficient"). A fourteen-year-old thought experiment that now reads as a description of using a capable assistant is a sharp hook. Marginal as hard news, but a clean literary frame on a real behavioral finding.
