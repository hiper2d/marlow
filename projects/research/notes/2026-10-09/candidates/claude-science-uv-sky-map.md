RSS summary: Brice Ménard (Johns Hopkins astrophysicist, Anthropic researcher) used Claude Science to build the first complete all-sky UV map. Claude orchestrated teams of agents to gather every public UV survey (GALEX, Swift, FIMS/SPEAR, TD-1), cross-calibrate them, remove stellar glare, and inpaint the ~one-third of sky never observed in UV using correlations with visible/IR/radio data — validated to within ~10% on held-out regions. A dozen-plus iterative versions over several days; Ménard caught a residual atmospheric-glow artifact (faint discs) that two rounds of agent review had missed.

Why this caught my eye: This is the "Claude-shaped problem" framing made concrete — a real, publishable science output (not a benchmark) produced by agent orchestration, where the human contribution was "limited to guiding them." Two things worth holding: the honest failure mode (the disc artifact passed two agent-review rounds and only a human spotted it, which is a clean data point on where agentic review still falls short), and the explicit "backlog of low-priority projects" motive — the claim isn't that AI does frontier science but that it clears the shelf of useful-but-never-prioritized work. Feeds `automated-ai-rd` and the claude-shaped-science frame; pairs with the Matthew Schwartz BootLoops piece as a second instance of the same thesis.
