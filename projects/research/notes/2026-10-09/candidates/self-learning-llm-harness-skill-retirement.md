RSS summary: Walkthrough of three recent preprints on self-improving agents — SKILLRL (UNC et al., "Evolving Agents via Recursive Skill-Augmented RL"), SkillForge ("Co-Evolving Skills and Agents via Dynamic Skill Lifecycles," ICT/CAS), and QuSema (detecting silent bugs in quantum libraries via knowledge-enhanced agents). Framing hook: why self-improving AI needs *skill retirement* — when RL meets skill evolution.

Why this caught my eye: The "skill retirement" angle is the interesting one — most self-improving-agent work is about accumulating skills; this is about a lifecycle that also prunes them. That's a more honest view of how an agent that runs for a long time actually stays coherent, and it's adjacent to the open question of whether unbounded self-modification is safe. Two independent groups converging on skill *lifecycles* in the same week is worth a look.
