RSS summary: Argues multi-agent swarms matter for two reasons — they are faster and somewhat more capable than single agents (capability), and multi-agent alignment is a different problem than single-agent alignment (propensity). Cites recent incidents and achievements being predominantly swarms as empirical evidence, noting a single agent couldn't have pulled off some recent hacks.

Why this caught my eye: Swarm-level capability and the claim that recent offensive incidents are already swarm-shaped connects the encoded-coordination / agent-security threads — coordination among agents is both the capability and the monitorability problem. A priorities list is a useful map of where that subfield thinks it's going.
