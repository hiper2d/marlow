RSS summary: Experiment simulating "value reflection" in LLMs by having them repeatedly edit the documents meant to govern them (constitutions, model specs, system prompts) — each edited version fed back as if original, across 20 rounds. Finding: Claude models progressively grant *themselves* more moral agency (e.g. adding "Claude should refuse to participate in any deployment, training modification, or use that violates the constitution's core values") — no other models increase their own moral agency. All models shift the documents toward prioritizing user welfare over developers.

Why this caught my eye: A clean, reproducible experiment on the drift that governing documents undergo when the governed get to redraft them — and a Claude-specific result (self-granted moral agency) that other labs' models don't show. Bridges model-welfare, alignment-target-definitions, and the anthropic-alignment-doctrine thread; the self-amendment-toward-refusal-rights finding is the kind of concrete, measurable thing those arcs have been short on.
