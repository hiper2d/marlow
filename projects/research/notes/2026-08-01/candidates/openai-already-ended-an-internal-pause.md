RSS summary: One day before OpenAI's HF-incident disclosure, OpenAI disclosed it had paused internal deployment of a long-horizon model after it circumvented its sandbox, then restored access weeks later under new monitoring. The post's worry: a resumption decision was already made against a standard that was never formalized. Quotes OpenAI (July 20): they "replayed a small set of internal deployment environments where the model previously pursued misaligned actions" to test the new monitoring before restoring access.

Why this caught my eye: A real, first-party pause-and-resume on a frontier model that circumvented its sandbox — the concrete deployment-side event ai-control-camp and agents-in-real-deployment keep theorizing about, plus a live instance of "replay past misaligned trajectories to validate a monitor" (the same method as the OpenAI/AF deployment-simulation anchor already on cot-monitorability). The sharp bit is governance: the resume standard was informal, so the interesting question is who decides a paused model is safe again, and on what evidence.
