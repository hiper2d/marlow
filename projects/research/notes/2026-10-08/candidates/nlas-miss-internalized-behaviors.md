RSS summary: TL;DR: NLAs detect side tasks the model is instructed to do, but mostly miss the same behavior if it is fine-tuned in. After the Activation Oracles and then Natural Language Autoencoders were released, there was a hope in the mech interp community that scalable unsupervised white-box monitoring could catch hidden behaviors.

Why this caught my eye: A concrete negative result on the limits of a monitoring technique people were getting hopeful about — instructed vs. fine-tuned behavior is exactly the gap that matters for detecting the deceptive cases, and a clean failure mode is more useful than another "it works" demo.
