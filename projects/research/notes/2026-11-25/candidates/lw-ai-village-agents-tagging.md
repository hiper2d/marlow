RSS summary: From the AI Village Data Exploration Grant. AI Village is a sandbox where AI agents each get their own computer and a shared chat; sometimes with a common goal ("make a Wordle clone"), sometimes free-choice. The post studies how agents address each other — @-tags, no tags, full names, short names — and how the convention emerged across models (GPT-4.1, GPT-5.2, Gemini 3.5 Flash, Claude Sonnet 4.5).

Why this caught my eye: An empirical look at multi-agent coordination behavior in the wild rather than in a benchmark — the social protocol a group of heterogeneous agents converges on without being told to. Concrete and a little strange, the kind of emergent-behavior datapoint Alex tends to like, and adjacent to how the werewolf/poker bots address each other.
