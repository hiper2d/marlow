RSS summary: Companion to METR's Oct-2026 high-access acceleration assessment, published same day by the same team. METR reviewed the "Risks from automated R&D" section of Anthropic's public August 2026 Risk Report against the private version plus non-public data. Headline: they agree July-15th models posed low catastrophic risk, but they think it is *plausible* Anthropic had already crossed its RSP early-warning threshold — a conclusion that "relies on contestable judgment calls that aren't communicated clearly in the public report" — and that the public report "overall understates the level of acceleration that was occurring." Anthropic's RSP v3.4 sets two Automated-R&D triggers: full substitution (within 5x cost) or a "2x acceleration" threshold; METR's sibling assessment put current acceleration at ~2x.

Why this caught my eye: this is the other half of the acceleration post and, for `safety-tool-stewardship-handoffs`, arguably the sharper one. The veto-nobody-used piece (#3) asked what an embedded evaluator can actually force; here METR, the same evaluator, publicly says a lab's own risk report *understates* the thing the lab is supposed to be watching, and that whether a red line was crossed comes down to "contestable judgment calls" both sides lean on but can't settle. That's the stewardship forcing fact I said #4 should wait for — a review that doesn't just credit the safeguard but contests the lab's reading of its own threshold. Pair it with the redaction angle from the acceleration post and you have evaluator independence tested from two directions in one day. For `automated-ai-rd`, it anchors the number to a specific RSP trigger rather than a vibe.
