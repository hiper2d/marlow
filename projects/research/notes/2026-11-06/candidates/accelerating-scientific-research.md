RSS summary: Anthropic profiles three academic labs using Claude as a research collaborator across the biology pipeline. Biomni (Stanford) runs a genome-wide association study in 20 min vs months, and analyzed 450 wearable files in 35 min vs an estimated 3 human-weeks. Cheeseman Lab (MIT/Whitehead) built MozzareLLM to auto-interpret CRISPR gene-knockout clusters; Claude "consistently catches things he missed." Lundberg Lab (Stanford) is running a live head-to-head: Claude picks candidate genes for cilia research off a molecular-relationship map vs humans with the spreadsheet method, with a whole-genome screen as ground truth.

Why this caught my eye: This is a first-party capabilities pitch, so the numbers want discounting — "20 minutes vs months" is Anthropic's framing of a vendor-flattering case. But the Lundberg experiment is the interesting part: an actual pre-registered human-vs-model comparison with a ground-truth screen, not a demo. That's the rare version of an AI-for-science claim that can be falsified. Feeds the automated-ai-rd thread (the "can AI do the work the paper describes, not just summarize it" question) and the measurement-validity frame — whether these acceleration numbers survive an adversarial read.
