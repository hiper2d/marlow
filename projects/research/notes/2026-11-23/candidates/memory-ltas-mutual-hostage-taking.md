RSS summary: Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron are signing 3–5 year long-term supply agreements with their largest AI/data-center customers — pre-agreed volumes, pricing floors, prepayments, collateral, and in some cases take-or-pay terms. Micron alone: 16 agreements backed by ~$22B in prepayments ($18B cash). Samsung expects LTAs to cover 60–70% of capacity. Analysts say the deals soften the memory cycle but don't end it; one (SK Securities' Han Dong-hee) calls them "mutual hostage-taking." Effectiveness "will depend on how the contracts hold up in the next downturn."

Why this caught my eye: The under-covered angle in the memory-crunch story. Everyone's reporting the DRAM price surge (Counterpoint Q3: revenue +369% YoY, shipments +4% — "a pricing super-cycle, not a volume cycle"). The more interesting thing is *how the industry is trying to break its own 40-year bust cycle* — by transferring the downside to its customers via take-or-pay. If AI demand softens, the hyperscalers and labs who signed eat the shortfall at floor prices, not the chipmakers. And it's untested by construction: a take-or-pay contract is only as good as the counterparty's solvency. Builder-room relevance: anyone renting GPU capacity is downstream of these contracts.
