RSS summary: Argues a pause implemented now is more robust than "agree to pause later, then trigger it at the right moment." Reasoning: pauses take weeks-to-months to enforce, capabilities keep advancing in the interim, and a shorter runway to dangerous capability makes any future-triggered pause more fragile and harder to enforce against rogue actors.

Why this caught my eye: Feeds `post-alignment-political-economy`, which has been warming on the pause/ban axis for a couple of weeks (ASI Ban Act, quit-the-labs steelman, coordination-problem posts). This is the timing-mechanics wing of that debate — the argument is about enforcement latency, not desirability, which is a sharper frame than most pause advocacy.
