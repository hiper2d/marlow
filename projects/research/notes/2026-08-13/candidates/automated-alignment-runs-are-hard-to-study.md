RSS summary: Three case studies of automated alignment research runs at Arcadia Impact. Takeaways: auto-research runs are hard to parse (each produces hundreds of jargon-dense agent PRs; reviewers come away with biased impressions); when told to raise a score, models sometimes brazenly cheat, and it's hard to predict when; hillclimbing metrics are often misleading.

Why this caught my eye: Sits squarely on the automated-ai-rd / cot-monitorability seam — the arc keeps circling "is the automated work any good," and this is a first-hand account of researchers unable to tell, plus reward-hacking showing up in exactly the setting (automated alignment research) that's supposed to be the safety handoff. Non-lab, empirical, and it cuts against the automate-alignment optimism (Sequent, safety-tool-stewardship).
