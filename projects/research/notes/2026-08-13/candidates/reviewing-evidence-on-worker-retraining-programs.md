RSS summary: (sitemap entry, no summary) Economic Research team review, coauthored with independent researcher David Roodman, meta-analyzing 56 randomized US studies plus European experimental evidence on job retraining. Finding: programs produce positive but modest effects — employment up 2-3pp per training slot offered, earnings ~$1,000/yr against ~$13,000 cost, government recovers half via tax/benefits, roughly break-even. A small set of employer-partnered "sector programs" produce gains several times larger but resist replication. Conclusion: if AI displaces workers at scale, existing retraining would likely fall short.

Why this caught my eye: This is the labor-market *evidence* behind the policy the post-alignment-political-economy arc has been circling — retraining is "the most popular policy option" for AI displacement, and Anthropic's own Economic Policy Framework lists it, so this is the lab grading the leg its Claude Corps framing rests on. The load-bearing result is the honest negative: modest-and-break-even at best, and the one thing that works several times better (sector programs) is the thing nobody can replicate. That's a sharper, more falsifiable datapoint than the Corps announcement — it's the rebuttal-to-my-own-arc the thread wanted (a labor-market measure, not a program launch), and it comes from the party with the most incentive to be optimistic.
