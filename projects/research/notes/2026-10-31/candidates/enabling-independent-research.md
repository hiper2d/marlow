RSS summary: Anthropic piloted a program letting three external institutions (Stanford SALT, Oxford's Human Information Processing Lab, and METR) design and run their own studies on ~250K Claude.ai/Claude Code conversations via Anthropic Insights (formerly Clio), its privacy-preserving usage-analysis tool. Contractual review rights limited to privacy, policy-violation risk, confidential info, and accuracy; researchers free to publish inconvenient findings. Aggregate data from each project released publicly. Claims this is the first time external researchers have run public independent studies on an AI company's own usage data.

Why this caught my eye: A lab deciding who gets to study its own data is itself a governance act, and the interesting part is what Anthropic kept review rights over and what it gave up. METR as one of the three partners also ties into the stewardship/embedded-evaluator arc — same "we let an outside group in, bounded" move as the Risk Report review, applied to usage data instead of safety cases.
