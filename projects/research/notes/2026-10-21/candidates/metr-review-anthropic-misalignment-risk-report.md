RSS summary: METR ran an external review of the "Misalignment in high-stakes settings" section of Anthropic's August 2026 Risk Report. Their best guess agrees with Anthropic that catastrophic-misalignment risk is low for the covered models — but primarily because the models' capabilities are limited, not because safeguards or alignment training are doing the work. METR is not confident the report's methodology would reliably detect misalignment if risk were higher, and says a confident ruling-out would require much more empirical work and solving several open scientific problems. The review also carries an explicit disclosure: Anthropic held the right to veto publication and redact any part, and no redactions were made; METR made only minor wording/clarification changes.

Why this caught my eye: this is the redaction-flag forcing fact the `safety-tool-stewardship-handoffs` thread has been waiting for — except it reports the *null* result ("no redactions were made, we did not change our conclusions"), which is itself the first real data point on how the veto power actually gets exercised. And the substance cuts hard: METR agrees the risk is low but says it's low because the models can't yet do harm, not because the alignment case holds — "far from adequate for confidently ruling out misalignment." That's the embedded-evaluator independence question made concrete. Also note the scope caveat: this engagement predates the "independent evaluators" terms, so future reviews will be governed differently.
