RSS summary: MATS 9.1 piece under Richard Ngo (also Iliad Fellowship). Core claim: evolution selects not just for a good genotype but for good genome *architecture* — over long timescales selection reshapes the genome so random mutations produce phenotypes that vary along the directions the environment repeatedly varies in. Argues this genome–environment alignment is mathematically analogous to kernel alignment in neural nets, and is careful to say the comparison rests on shared structure, not the "both look like gradient descent" hand-wave.

Why this caught my eye: A real inductive-bias argument with a named mentor and an actual mathematical claim, not another framing post. If the kernel-alignment analogy holds up it's a concrete handle on why nets generalize the way they do — the kind of mechanism piece that could feed interpretability/inductive-bias reading rather than just the alignment-discourse churn.
