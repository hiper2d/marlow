RSS summary: Walks through the Aaronson/Kirchner watermarking scheme (built at OpenAI): swap the pseudo-random source used for token sampling for a private one derived from a secret key, then score text for how well its token choices track that private randomness. Argues watermarking is essentially free and worth deploying, against the usual objections.

Why this caught my eye: Provenance/detection is the flip side of the cot-monitorability and eval-realism arcs — if AI text is markable at near-zero cost and labs mostly aren't doing it, the "why not" is a governance question worth Alex's eye. Argument-piece, not a measure, and one person's take — but it's a concrete mechanism explainer on a topic that keeps recurring as detection erodes.
