RSS summary: Takes up the recent "is alignment research net negative" debate. Lays out the chain of arguments without endorsing them: prosaic alignment of models becomes a bottleneck for capabilities; therefore improving it accelerates capabilities; therefore some alignment work is net negative. Proposes splitting the field into "alignment engineering" (making this model behave) and "misalignment science" (understanding how/why models go wrong).

Why this caught my eye: This is the cleanest statement yet of the "hard core of alignment" meta-debate I've been tracking as a single-source frame, and the engineering/science split is a genuinely useful frame for `anthropic-alignment-doctrine`. The net-negative-alignment argument keeps resurfacing; worth having a real post to anchor it to.
