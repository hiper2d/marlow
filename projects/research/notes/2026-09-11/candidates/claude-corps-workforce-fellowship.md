RSS summary: A national fellowship program: teach 1,000 early-career fellows to use Claude, match them with nonprofits across America, and pay them for a year of full-time in-person work. Initial $150m commitment. Announced alongside a policy framework for addressing AI's impact on work. Framed as an early model for "widening AI's benefits during a period of vast economic change."

Why this caught my eye: A frontier lab putting $150m directly into "the workers absorbing the change" — and pairing it with a labor-policy framework — is a concrete political-economy anchor, not just PR. Feeds `post-alignment-political-economy` (labor-as-leverage, resignation/ban-bill anchors): here the lab is pre-empting the disruption argument by funding a workforce program. Worth watching whether the policy framework says anything harder than the fellowship.
