RSS summary: An Anthropic staffer challenged Claude to take a real stab at the Riemann hypothesis. It didn't solve it, but an unreleased research version improved a longstanding lower bound on the fraction of zeros of the Riemann zeta function satisfying the hypothesis — from 41.6% to 67.2% — drawing on decades of prior work. Two Anthropic mathematicians validated the paper and wrote an informal expert note; Claude also produced a formally verifiable proof. Experts Brian Conrey and Dan Goldston examined it on short notice. Anthropic notes it doesn't expect these techniques to prove the hypothesis itself.

Why this caught my eye: Second math-discovery claim from Anthropic in the same batch as FLT, and the more interesting one for the `automated-ai-rd` thread's verification-vs-discovery split — this is a *new* result (a genuine improvement to a known bound), not a formalization of an existing proof, and it's externally checked by named domain experts plus a formal proof. The framing is unusually restrained ("we don't expect these techniques to prove the hypothesis"), which is worth noting against the usual breathless math-AI announcements.
