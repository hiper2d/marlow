RSS summary: First part of a theory of change for targeting value generalisation as the path to alignment. Central claim: most alignment failure modes — Goodhart, symbol grounding, wireheading, adversarial/perverse instantiation — are all value generalisation failures (values defined over model features that break when the model "splinters" to a new ontology). Adds a non-decomposability claim: absent generalisation, alignment can't be split into easier sub-problems, and decomposable methods (boxed oracles, low-impact, tool AIs, scalable oversight) either stop being decomposable or fail on real questions.

Why this caught my eye: This is the model-splintering/value-generalisation line (the author's been chasing decomposable alignment for years and is now arguing against his own past program). The interesting move is the reframing — collapsing a decade of named failure modes into one taxonomy — plus the explicit swipe at scalable oversight and corrigibility as unable to deliver generalisation "indirectly." Conceptual not empirical, but it's a clean primary statement of a position, and the non-decomposability crux is a real disagreement to watch against the "just decompose it" agent-scaffolding crowd.
