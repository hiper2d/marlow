RSS summary: It's strange that LLMs have (plausibly) solved a millennium problem before a single one has unambiguously solved the Turing test — i.e. learned to write duplicitously or convincingly in other styles. The post argues for a strong definition of the test and questions whether current models actually meet it.

Why this caught my eye: Flips the usual "LLMs passed the Turing test" claim by raising the bar to convincing stylistic duplicity — writing *as* someone else well enough to fool a judge — and notes models are oddly weak there despite being strong elsewhere. The capability-asymmetry framing (hard math solved, convincing impersonation not) is a sharper hook than the usual benchmark triumphalism.
