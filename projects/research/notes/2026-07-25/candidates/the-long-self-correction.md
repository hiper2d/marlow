RSS summary: Proposes "The Long Self-Correction" as an alternative to AI Pause and Long Reflection. Both existing framings assume humans are fit to build/oversee/serve as alignment targets for powerful AI; the author (reads as Wei Dai) argues the deeper problem is that humans are currently too flawed — no workable moral framework, bad at philosophy/long-horizon strategy, badly calibrated about that incompetence, morality-as-status-game, positional/zero-sum values, easy to manipulate. Hope rests on humans having mysteriously made progress on these issues over long timescales; the goal is to preserve the environment that allows that snowballing correction and deny anyone the power to permanently derail it.

Why this caught my eye: A framing move, not a result, but it names something the alignment-target-definitions arc keeps circling — that "align to humans" quietly assumes the target is fit for purpose. Reframing the problem as humans-not-being-ready-yet rather than AI-not-being-aligned-yet is a clean fork worth an anchor.
