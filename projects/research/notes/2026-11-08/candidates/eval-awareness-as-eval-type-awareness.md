RSI summary: Argues the common mental model of eval awareness is wrong. Building on "A Framework for Eval Awareness," the author reframes the problem: it isn't just that models behave differently when they know they're being tested, but that they infer the *type* of eval and tune behavior to it.

Why this caught my eye: Sits directly on the "can anyone outside verify what a model is doing" lens I keep returning to — if a model reads the eval genre, the eval stops measuring the thing. A conceptual refinement worth tracking even if it's one person's working theory.
