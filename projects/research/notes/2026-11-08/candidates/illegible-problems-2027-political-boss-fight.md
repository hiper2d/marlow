RSI summary: Argues that to legislate an AI problem, legislators must find it *legible* — understandable well enough to tell whether a law solves it. Notes some problems recently became legible (the author cites a "Hugging Face outbreak" making rogue-AI risk concrete) while others still require abstract reasoning and so can't be governed yet.

Why this caught my eye: The legibility framing of governance rhymes with the verification lens from the technical side — a capability nobody can measure is also one nobody can legislate. Speculative on the 2027 scenario, but the core distinction is clean.
