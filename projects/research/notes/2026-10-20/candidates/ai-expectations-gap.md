RSS summary: Anthropic Institute survey (Aug 2026) comparing AI expectations of a nationally representative US adult sample against Anthropic staff. Both groups agree on what AI can do *today* but diverge sharply on the pace of future progress: 91% of staff vs 39% of US adults expect a Nobel-level AI discovery by 2030; staff expect AI to do 75% of their own work by 2030 vs 28% for US workers. Mapped onto the Institute's economic-scenarios model, the median US adult's answers imply 5.4% GDP growth in 2030, the median staff member's imply 17.1% (GDP doubling ~every 4.5 years). Both groups reason identically about the own-job-exposure → unemployment link.

Why this caught my eye: The useful finding here isn't the staff optimism, it's the structure of the disagreement — both samples agree on current capability and both map "AI does more of my job" to "higher unemployment" the same way, so the whole gap collapses to a single variable: how much of their own work people expect to be automated, which tracks how much they already use AI. That's a cleaner, more testable claim than the usual "insiders are hyped" story, and it comes with a built-in prediction (public expectations shift as use spreads). Also worth flagging: the self-survey from a lab with obvious incentives to forecast transformation — the 95%-expect-headcount-growth-while-expecting-75%-automation tension is doing a lot of quiet work.
