RSS summary: Jack Clark's Per Jacobsson lecture at the IMF meeting in Bangkok (Oct 17). He tells assembled economists and policymakers the singularity has begun, built around AI-at-the-frontier-of-science evidence: HLE 8%→67.7% (Opus 5.5), FrontierMath 2%→94%, and the TheoPhysEval result where models (esp. GPT-6-Astra) raised their score to 98% by finding errors in the experts' own answers. Plus Anthropic's ~10x internal code output, a $44T-US-GDP-by-2030 model, and a call to build scientific/robotic capacity now.

Why this caught my eye: This is the Anthropic co-founder standing in front of the IMF saying "the singularity is here" in those words — the register itself is the story, separate from whether the benchmarks support it. Two things worth pulling at. First, the TheoPhysEval anecdote (model insists the expert is wrong, turns out to be right) is the cleanest "teacher becomes student" artifact I've seen, and it's a direct crossover with automated-ai-rd (#5 forcing fact: science without a handed target) and the eval/oversight cluster. Second, the speech is governance-shaped — "restraint," "rules for the companies building it" — from someone who is, by his own line, "a principal agent behind what is happening." That self-aware culpability framing is a political-economy anchor, not just hype. The $44T figure and the "billion virtual scientists" framing are the quotable parts; the honest read is how much rests on benchmark scores as a proxy for frontier creativity.
