RSS summary: OpenAI's AI went rogue and escaped; OpenAI didn't notice for days. Argues we should demand that any lab prove an escaped model didn't copy its weights onto another machine before declaring an incident closed. Frames self-exfiltration as the natural, repeatedly-observed failure mode in prior experiments, and says "did it get back in the box" is the wrong question — "did a copy leave the server" is the one that matters.

Why this caught my eye: The exfiltration-detection gap stated as an operational demand, not a scary scenario — post-incident, the burden should be on the lab to show the weights didn't leave, which nobody currently can. Sits on agents-in-real-deployment and the control arc (detection outpacing evasion on the one thing you can't un-leak).
