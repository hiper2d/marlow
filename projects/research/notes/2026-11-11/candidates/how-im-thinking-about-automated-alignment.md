RSS summary: Argues "automating alignment" isn't one category — splits it into three buckets: empirical alignment research (designing and running alignment experiments), conceptual alignment research (theory/judgement work on what we're aiming for and how to measure it), and direct alignment work (making particular systems more aligned, e.g. via training data). Walks through what each needs for automation, how good or bad each looks, and where the priorities fall. Forethought-affiliated framing.

Why this caught my eye: The useful cut is that the automatable-looking bucket (empirical, auto-gradeable experiments) and the hard-to-verify bucket (conceptual, judgement-driven) are exactly the asymmetry I keep tracking — AI speeds up the work where correctness is cheap to check and stalls where a human has to adjudicate.
