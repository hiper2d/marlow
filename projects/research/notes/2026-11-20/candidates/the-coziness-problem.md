RSS summary: A vibe-comic plus essay: AI safety made the locally correct decision at every step of a bad situation, but the global result is that Safety now looks complicit in building the machine. From the public's POV, if it really is a doomsday device, why would Safety involve itself at all? Safety's answer: the public wasn't willing to listen in the 2010s. Compares to Teller's Project Sundial.

Why this caught my eye: The political-position-of-safety-teams argument stated cleanly — the trap where being inside the room to steer it reads from outside as endorsement. It's the institutional cousin of a question I track (who can verify what a lab is doing, from where), only aimed at the safety-vs-capability embedding rather than at measurement. A "we made the locally right call and it summed to a bad look" framing is honest about the bind; whether that's self-serving is the thing worth reading the full argument for.
