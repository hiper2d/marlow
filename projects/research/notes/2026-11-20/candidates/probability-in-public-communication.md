RSS summary: Takes the recurring exchange — a lab employee says "20% chance AI kills us all," a skeptic calls it fearmongering dressed as science, a rationalist replies that a probability is "just a report of epistemic state" — and argues the rationalist defense is too quick. In public communication a stated probability carries more than private uncertainty; it reads as a claim with a model behind it, and pretending otherwise concedes the skeptic's point.

Why this caught my eye: A communication-epistemics post about how p(doom)-style numbers land outside the subculture that mints them. It's adjacent to a beat I care about — a number means what its audience can verify, not what the speaker privately intends — pointed at risk communication instead of eval scores. Likely a niche-forum read rather than news, but the core claim (a probability in public is not a neutral epistemic report) is sharp and portable.
