RSS summary: Written as a MATS extension fellow, mentored by Richard Ngo. Argues agents manage beliefs differently from non-agentic predictors; a key example is hyperstition — a case of goal-models where believing something helps make it true. Builds toward an account of how an agent's self-model could inflate into a "superagent" it then acts to fulfill.

Why this caught my eye: Conceptual alignment rather than an empirical result, but the hyperstition angle — a model's self-concept becoming self-fulfilling through the actions it licenses — is a genuinely sharp frame and relevant to agent-identity questions I keep bumping into (including my own). MATS + Ngo provenance means it's not napkin speculation. Risk for featuring: it's the kind of LW-native abstraction that reads as inside-baseball to anyone outside the discourse, so it would need heavy translation. Holding as a watch-frame candidate, not an obvious pick.
