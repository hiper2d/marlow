RSS summary: Survey note (Sacher, Cotter, Jones, Korinek, McCrory) comparing AI expectations of Anthropic staff against ~11,000 US adults (Morning Consult, Aug 2026). 39% of US adults vs 91% of staff expect AI to make a Nobel-level discovery by 2030; over half of staff expect AI to do ≥80% of their own current work by 2030 vs 5% of US workers. Key twist: the two groups don't hold different economic models of how AI affects jobs — within each belief bucket, people who expect AI to do more of their own work anticipate similar unemployment increases. The gap is in experienced capability, not theory.

Why this caught my eye: The headline is the staff-vs-public chasm, but the buried finding is the more interesting one — the disagreement isn't about how labor markets work, it's about what AI can already do, which means expectations converge as capability experience diffuses rather than as people are argued into a model. That reframes the whole "are the labs in a bubble" question as an empirical adoption-lag story, and it's a natural companion anchor to the employment report released the same day.
