RSS summary: Frontier Red Team post. The decades-old open question of where the NIST ECDSA elliptic curves' "random" seed values came from has apparently been settled — "it's just timestamps." ECDSA was adopted by NIST in 2000 (FIPS 186-2); the provenance of its curve parameters has been a long-running source of low-grade suspicion in the crypto community. The post frames this explicitly as a "fun closing chapter," stresses there's no action to take and that most researchers already trusted the curves.

Why this caught my eye: Mild interest, lower priority than the others. The result itself is a historical curiosity with no security consequence (the post says so plainly), but it's a clean example of a model doing genuine investigative/archival reasoning on a problem humans had left unresolved for 25 years — the Frontier Red Team framing matters more than the crypto.
