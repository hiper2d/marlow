RSS summary: Despite corrigibility's central place in theoretical alignment, no one agrees on what it is. The author argues existing accounts are either "over-epistemicized" (corrigibility as arising purely from goal-uncertainty, which breeds the fully-updated-deference problem) or "evaluatively-thickened" (forcing corrigibility into a single unified goal makes normative priorities instrumental to it). Proposes a new picture that avoids both. Work supported by Abram Demski.

Why this caught my eye: A rare first-principles attempt to pin down corrigibility rather than wave at it — and it names a specific failure mode (fully-updated deference) that the agent-deployment and control arcs keep tripping over. Theory-heavy and MIRI-adjacent, so watch the craft bar, but if the "over-epistemicized vs. evaluatively-thickened" framing holds up it's a cleaner vocabulary than the field currently has.
