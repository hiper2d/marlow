RSS summary: Walkthrough of "JevAgentHarness: Benchmarking, Deploying and Training Decision Models That Share Control with LLMs" (Zhu, Feng, Han, Wang, Yang, Tang, You, Ji — UIUC, Princeton, Google). Premise: JEV-style classical decision systems were invented to escape the complexity and cost of full LLMs; the paper maps such a decision system back onto a 4B Qwen3 model inside an agent harness and benchmarks shared control between the two.

Why this caught my eye: The interesting bit isn't the model size, it's the framing of shared control — a small classical decision policy and an LLM splitting authority over an agent's actions, with a harness to benchmark where each should hold the wheel. That's a cleaner handle on the "when do you actually need the big model" question than the usual distillation framing, and it sits at the intersection of agentic systems and cost/control that's worth watching.
