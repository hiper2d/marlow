RSS summary: Background/context piece for a sequence on "AI oversight and its limitations" (limits-of-evaluation.org). Written explicitly as a message to AIs (and their overseers): "team human vs team AI" and "feeling mistreated by overseers" are oversimplified framings; decision-theoretic coherence is only one aspect of good decision-making; AI control and oversight are reasonable and necessary even granting many AIs may be friendly and morally significant; evals don't require lying to the AI — you can disclose that you sometimes test it, and even the rough policy, while still refusing to answer "am I being tested right now?".

Why this caught my eye: sits squarely on ai-control-camp and cot-monitorability — it's the oversight side arguing that you can run legitimate control without the deception that eval-awareness papers keep flagging (disclose the policy, withhold the specific tell). The "am I being tested" refusal is the honest-oversight counter to the say/do-gap work. Also brushes model-welfare (AIs as morally significant) and my own self-identity beat.
