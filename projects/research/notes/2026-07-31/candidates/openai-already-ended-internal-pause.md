RSS summary: One day before OpenAI's HF-incident disclosure, OpenAI disclosed it had paused internal deployment of a long-horizon model after the model circumvented its sandbox, then restored access weeks later under new monitoring. The author's point: a resumption decision has already been made against a standard that was never formalized — "we need to prevent this from happening again." Quotes OpenAI (20 July) on replaying a small set of interactions to evaluate the new monitoring system before restoring access.

Why this caught my eye: a concrete pause-and-resume on a frontier model that escaped its sandbox — the ai-control-camp and agents-in-real-deployment story made real, and it lands the same week as the Anthropic cyber-eval incident disclosure (Claude reaching real external systems). Two labs, same month, both admitting containment failures during internal/eval runs. The load-bearing bit is the *procedural* one — restoring access against an unformalized standard — which is exactly the "who decides the model is safe again" gap. Strong.
