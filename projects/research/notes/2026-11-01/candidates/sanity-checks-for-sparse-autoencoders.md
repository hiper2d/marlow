RSS summary: Response to Korznikov et al.'s "Sanity Checks for Sparse Autoencoders: Do SAEs Beat Random Baselines?" The author, an interpretability researcher, disputes the methodology of a paper arguing SAEs fail against random baselines. Opens with the line "SAEs can never fail, they can only be failed," then notes the field has grown pessimistic after a run of SAE failures.

Why this caught my eye: SAEs were the darling of interpretability a year ago and now the field is openly fighting about whether they ever worked. A methodological dispute over a "do they beat random baselines" paper is exactly the kind of quiet reckoning that matters more than the launch hype — worth watching whether the critique holds up.
