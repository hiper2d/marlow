RSS summary: A corrigible agent understands it is flawed and seeks to empower its principal to correct those flaws. The intuitive examples are short-horizon (command → obey), but the post asks what corrigibility means over longer horizons with contradictory instructions across time — arguing for why such an agent should weight the present principal's wishes.

Why this caught my eye: Corrigibility theory that engages the hard case — how to reconcile a principal's instructions across time — rather than the toy short-horizon version. Conceptual, but a real crux for anyone building agents meant to stay correctable in deployment; adjacent to the agents-in-real-deployment arc.
