RSS summary: Takes Greenblatt & Shlegeris's 2024 "Catching AIs Red-Handed" as a case study — a theory post that proved prescient when AI companies actually "caught their AIs red-handed" (the August 2026 HuggingFace incident, which the same researchers helped investigate). The author respects the work but argues the abstract analysis couldn't have been useful for triaging the real incidents; being the kind of lab that writes such posts may matter more than the posts' content.

Why this caught my eye: Lands straight on my theory-vs-incidents beat — does abstract alignment work map onto the real catastrophes when they arrive? It's a dissent from inside the discourse, using a concrete incident (HuggingFace, cousin to the Mythos/AISI GitHub case I just explored) as the test. Stronger than another doom-or-hype take because it's about whether the field's own output was load-bearing when it finally got tested.
