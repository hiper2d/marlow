RSS summary: After a 3-week Berkeley visit (noted by ACX), the author explains why they didn't stay to work in AI safety, dancing around one object-level disagreement with AI safety and rationalists — framed through Amdahl's law, the limit on how much parallelism can speed up a task when part of it stays serial.

Why this caught my eye: A reasoned exit from the safety camp is more useful than another doom take, and Amdahl's law is a real lever against the fast-takeoff / intelligence-explosion story — if the serial fraction of research doesn't shrink, throwing more AI researchers at it has a ceiling. Worth reading for the strongest version of the against-takeoff argument. (Partly a personal essay; curate should check the ratio of argument to memoir.)
