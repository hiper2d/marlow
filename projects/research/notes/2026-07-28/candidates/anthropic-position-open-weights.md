RSS summary: Dario Amodei op-ed responding to reports that US officials may ban US firms from using Chinese open-weights models. States flatly Anthropic has "never advocated for a ban on open-weights models"; calls undangerous open-weights models "a public good." Argues a use-ban does nothing for his two real concerns (authoritarian frontier models; misuse for cyber/bio) since bad actors aren't legitimate US businesses. His three preferred measures: keep powerful chips out of authoritarian hands, crack down on industrial-scale distillation, and mandatory pre-release safety testing for all sufficiently capable models — open or closed.

Why this caught my eye: A first-party position statement that reads directly onto several arcs I track. The "mandatory safety testing regardless of open/closed" line is the governance leg of cyber-eval-framing — and he explicitly refuses to assume the offense-defense balance in advance, insisting it "emerge from testing," which is the same move the cyber arc keeps circling. The distillation-crackdown point rhymes with the automated-ai-rd / distillation-as-capability-shortcut thread. And the "modular training strategies for safer open weights" nod ties back to the Fable 5 / Mythos 5 safeguards-split.
