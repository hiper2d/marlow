RSS summary: A benchmark for whether AI models can tell novel jokes that actually make a person laugh, pitched as a robust general-intelligence indicator (unlike coding tasks). Informally run over months: no model has produced laughter above the accidental base rate; the chart shows all bars at zero. Frontier models (GPT-5.6 Sol, Fable) occasionally manage a smile. Argument: humor requires modeling another mind's false inferences while avoiding them yourself, which is IQ-hard and resists the memory/pattern shortcuts other benchmarks allow.

Why this caught my eye: A capability eval built specifically to be un-gameable by scale-and-memory — the "is the benchmark measuring the thing" question the automated-ai-rd / proxy-quality cluster keeps hitting, from an unusually concrete angle. Marginal, but it's a real eval with a point, not filler.
