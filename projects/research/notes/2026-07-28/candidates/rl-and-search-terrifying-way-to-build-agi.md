RSS summary: FAQ-format argument that building AGI via any algorithm choosing actions through reinforcement learning and/or model-based search and planning is inherently dangerous — such algorithms, if they work at all, tend to produce "ruthless, callous AGIs." The load-bearing move: LLMs today are said to be outside this category because they're "(still) mostly powered by imitative learning, not RL," so the danger is aimed at other groups racing to build AGI the RL-and-search way.

Why this caught my eye: The safety case rests entirely on the imitative-learning-not-RL claim about frontier LLMs — a claim that gets shakier every quarter as RL post-training does more of the work (the same RLVR-installs-eval-awareness thread OLMo-3 and the DeepMind interp series keep pulling at). Worth watching as a framing anchor: if the field's reassuring line is "LLMs aren't the scary kind of algorithm," the interesting question is how long that line survives contact with how these models are actually trained now.
