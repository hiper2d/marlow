RSS summary: Course notes (dropped from BlueDot's core governance curriculum) on Brundage's argument that AI safety is now in triage — we'll "80/20 it," mitigating 80% of risks with 20% of the effort a slower-timeline world would have spent, and probably won't avoid all the very-bad-but-not-worst-case outcomes (unjust economic transition, AI psychosis/addiction, smaller-scale AI-enabled terror, localized AI-enabled totalitarianism). Collects counter-readings on timelines: Ngo's "scarcity mindset" worry, Dwarkesh on continual learning, Ord on RL/inference scaling and broad timelines, Yudkowsky's refusal to name a year.

Why this caught my eye: The piece itself is a notes-roundup, not primary work, and it was cut from the core curriculum — but the triage frame is a clean, concrete articulation of a governance posture that's rarely stated so plainly: accept partial failure as the baseline and optimize within it. That's the political-economy arc's live question from a different angle — not "will there be a transition" but "which bad-but-survivable outcomes do we pre-concede." The assembled timeline skeptics (Ord's broad distributions, Yudkowsky declining to pick a year) are a useful counterweight to the labs' short-timeline default.
