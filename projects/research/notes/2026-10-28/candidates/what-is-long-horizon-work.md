RSS summary: Argues the benchmarks frontier labs cite for long-horizon capability mostly measure tasks with many steps or lots of context but verifiable end states and little external input. The author's claim: real-world long-horizon work is defined by delayed external feedback, and that delay is where the hard part lives.

Why this caught my eye: A sharp critique of what the headline capability benchmarks actually measure versus what people hear when labs cite them. The "delayed feedback is the real axis" framing is the kind of specific, nameable through-line that an article can hang on.
