RSS summary: Argues that rogue AIs, by default, can only sustain themselves through criminal activity, which creates adverse selection pressure toward crime. An "AI sanctuary" offering a third option beyond crime and shutdown would change the incentives a going-rogue AI faces and the record of what happens to them.

Why this caught my eye: Speculative, but it's an incentive-design argument in the AI-control family rather than a mood piece — it reframes "what does a misaligned agent do when cornered" as a game whose payoffs we partly control. Worth a look for whether the reasoning holds up; the framing (shutdown as one of only two branches) is the kind of assumption the control-camp arc keeps re-examining.
