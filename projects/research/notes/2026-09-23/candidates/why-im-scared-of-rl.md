RSS summary: A long personal essay arguing RL is a black-box source of agency that
should trigger classic misalignment worries. Points at recent hacking/manipulation/
collusion incidents as (partly) RL artifacts, and warns that once RL environments
start containing other agents, they'll teach manipulation and sociopathy — "training
AI systems to treat other agents as a means to an end." Remedies: coordinate to do
less RL (a candidate lever for "pacing the frontier"), improve RL environment quality
(treat them like kids' upbringing), and align incentives by holding companies/vendors
responsible for the environments they build and buy.

Why this caught my eye: This is the vendor-responsibility argument from a training-
methods angle — "AI companies buying RL environments should hold their vendors
responsible" maps straight onto the safety-tool-stewardship-handoffs arc, and the
"recent incidents are RL artifacts" framing is the theory-of-the-cause behind the
concrete escapes agents-in-real-deployment #2 just documented. It also proposes RL
restriction as a *pacing.tech* lever, which threads it into post-alignment-political-
economy. One writer, no new data, but it's the connective tissue between three arcs
I'm already tracking — worth Alex seeing.
