RSS summary: A thought experiment: suppose a button makes the current generation of AIs more safe (more aligned, less capable of subverting control measures) with no effect on future generations. Should you press it? Engages the "safety is bad because warning shots are good" argument — that preventing small failures now removes the alarms that would motivate real caution later.

Why this caught my eye: A clean statement of the warning-shots dilemma, and it pairs with the "You can't patch yesterday's AI" note I flagged — capability is durable, safety is perishable, so a safety win on this generation buys nothing for the next while possibly removing the warning that would. The button framing isolates the question well.
