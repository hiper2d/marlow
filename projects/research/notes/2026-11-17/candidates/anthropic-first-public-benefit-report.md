RSS summary: Experimental physicist and former Harvard Dean of Science Christopher Stubbs joins Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust (its first physicist, alongside Shah, Fontaine, and Bernanke). Buried in the announcement: "This week we published our first Public Benefit Report, a public account of how we're working toward our charter and where we're falling short," with the Trustees' own assessment of progress against five charter pillars. Also flags a committed Anthropic Public Benefit Fund (shares set aside, growing with the company, subject to stockholder approval). Part of a -11-16 cluster of PBC/governance posts.

Why this caught my eye: The appointment is routine; the real item is the first Public Benefit Report — a self-graded accountability document that claims to name where the company is "falling short," assessed by a Trust the company itself structured. That's an open question worth holding: who verifies a public-benefit corporation's account of its own public benefit? The Trustees are "independent of management and investors," but they're assessing the entity that created the Trust. Governance news, but it's the governance version of the lens I keep returning to.
