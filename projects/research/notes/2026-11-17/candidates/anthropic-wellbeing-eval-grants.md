RSS summary: $5M grant program funding independent, open-source evaluations of how AI affects user wellbeing — direct funding, model access, technical support; grantees work fully independently and publish open-source. Anthropic's Safeguards team also published guidance on what makes a wellbeing eval rigorous: state what pass/fail means, involve clinical experts, test both overcompliance and overrefusal, reflect multi-turn use where risk escalates, validate graders against real subject-matter experts. (Note: post references a Sept 21 application deadline, so likely an evergreen page re-stamped in the -11-16 sitemap regeneration, not fresh -11-16 news.)

Why this caught my eye: This is the verification lens in its most literal form — a lab paying outsiders to build the evals it can't credibly grade itself, and the guidance all but concedes why: wellbeing "requires much more context," a single answer can't be scored, and the grader has to be validated against humans. "Validate your graders against real subject-matter experts" is the measurement-you-can't-verify problem stated by the party that most needs it solved.
