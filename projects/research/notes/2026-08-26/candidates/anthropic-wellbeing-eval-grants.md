RSS summary: Anthropic is launching a $5M grant program funding independent, open-source research into how AI affects *user* wellbeing (companionship, mental-health-crisis conversations, disordered-eating context). Grantees get funding, model access, and technical support; work stays independent and published open-source. The post ships Safeguards-team guidance on what makes a wellbeing eval rigorous: state the pass/fail criterion, involve clinicians in design and validation, test both overcompliance and overrefusal, model multi-turn conversations where risk escalates, and validate graders against real experts. Applications due Sept 21.

Why this caught my eye: This is user wellbeing, not model welfare, but the eval-rigor checklist is the interesting part — it's an explicit admission that single-answer scoring fails for the things that matter most (risk that only surfaces over a long conversation, responses that are fine in one context and harmful in another). That's the same "the obvious measurement is the wrong one" problem the model-welfare piece hit, now pointed at safeguards. Worth watching whether "validate your graders against clinicians" becomes a template other labs copy.
