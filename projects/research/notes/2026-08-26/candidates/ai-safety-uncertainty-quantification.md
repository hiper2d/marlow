RSS summary: A McGill math/stats PhD candidate working on scalable Bayesian methods reframes their own research as AI safety. Argues UQ — knowing when a model doesn't know — is an under-connected bridge between mainstream statistics and the safety agenda, having assumed until recently they were already "working on AI safety."

Why this caught my eye: The interesting angle isn't the UQ tutorial, it's the meta-point — a working statistician discovering the safety field's boundary from the outside, and the safety field's tendency to not count adjacent rigorous work as "safety." Could be thin (personal-experience framing), but the "who counts as doing safety research" question is a recurring live wire and this is a concrete instance of it.
