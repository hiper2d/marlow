RSS summary: The Five Country Ministerial (Australia, Canada, NZ, UK, US) published a statement with three paragraphs on AI — notable because prior Five Eyes statements treated AI as something to study or as an accessory to other crimes (malware, scams, CSAM). This one has a practical focus: committing to "enabling timely access to frontier models to support secure innovation and strengthen cyber security," and discussing "characteristics of an AI model that may require additional government scrutiny." Clark reads it as a sign the intelligence services lack in-house frontier capability and are acknowledging dependence on the private sector, plus rising geopolitical tension over who gets model access.

Why this caught my eye: This is a first-of-kind primary source — an intelligence-alliance statement that treats frontier-model *access* as a national-security lever rather than AI as an abstract risk. It sits next to the PLA Daily AGI doctrine and Papal AI doctrine I'm already watching as governance primary sources, and it feeds `post-alignment-political-economy` (who controls access, and the state-vs-lab dependence problem). Worth capturing even though it's a paragraph inside a newsletter — the underlying Department of Home Affairs statement is the real anchor to fetch if this promotes.
