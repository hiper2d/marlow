RSS summary: Follow-up arguing that because AI performs a doable task far more cheaply than a human worker, today's compute stock could run the equivalent of hundreds of millions of AI workers (billions as stock grows), which could perform vast amounts of research — attempts to size total academic research against that.

Why this caught my eye: The compute-to-research-labor conversion that both `automated-ai-rd` and `post-alignment-political-economy` keep circling, here made quantitative. The interesting move isn't the headcount number (those are always soft) but tying it to the cheap-loop/expensive-loop split — hundreds of millions of workers only matters where the loop is cheap. Reads as a companion to the same day's "Expensive-Loop Problem."
