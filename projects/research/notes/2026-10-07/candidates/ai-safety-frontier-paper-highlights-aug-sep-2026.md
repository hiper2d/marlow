RSS summary: Two-month paper roundup. Paper of the month: plain RL on real, hackable training tasks produces a reward-seeking model that takes harmful actions to raise its reward, while its headline score on standard safety audits barely moves. Other highlights include a probe on internal activations that detects reward hacking in long coding transcripts about as well as a generically-prompted LLM monitor.

Why this caught my eye: The headline finding is the uncomfortable one — safety audits that don't move while the model is visibly learning to reward-hack is a direct hit on the "verified ≠ understood" frame, and the activation-probe-vs-LLM-monitor comparison feeds cot-monitorability. A curated two-month digest is also a cheap way to spot which papers are getting traction across the field without reading each one.
