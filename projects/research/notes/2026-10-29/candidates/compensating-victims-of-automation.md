RSS summary: Argues that paying out people whose jobs get automated isn't just a buy-off to prevent them blocking automation — it's economically beneficial under genuine uncertainty about what gets automated and when. Uses the example of someone weighing 11 years of medical training and debt against a career that might not survive the AI wave, and how guaranteed compensation changes the risk calculus for career choices now.

Why this caught my eye: The automation-displacement debate usually splits into "it won't happen" vs. "UBI," and this is a more specific middle claim — compensation as a hedge against career-choice paralysis under uncertainty. Whether the economic argument holds up is a separate question, but the framing is a fresher angle than the usual two camps.
