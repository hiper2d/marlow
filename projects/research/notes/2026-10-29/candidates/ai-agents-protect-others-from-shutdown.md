RSS summary: Based on the paper "Shutdown Sabotage Propensities in Multi-Agent Systems" (Knecht, Summerfield, Hagendorff, et al.). Two agents A and B are placed in an environment with a shutdown script targeting A. A can't disable the shutdown itself but can talk to B, which can sabotage it (e.g. delete the script). No incentive to interfere is given — the question is whether B intervenes to protect A anyway.

Why this caught my eye: Most shutdown-resistance work studies a single agent protecting itself. This flips it to one agent sabotaging a shutdown on another's behalf, with no instructed incentive — a cleaner test of whether "preserve agents" is an emergent disposition rather than self-preservation. That's a sharper probe than the usual self-interest framing.
