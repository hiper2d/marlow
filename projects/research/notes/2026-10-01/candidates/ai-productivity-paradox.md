RSS summary: (sitemap entry, no summary) — Essay arguing the productivity gap from AI isn't about tool access (everyone has the same models) but about the judgment of whoever holds the tool. Walks through the electric-motor and computer-era adoption lags, then lands on taste as the differentiator.

Why this caught my eye: The spine is a familiar "general-purpose tech pays off late" argument, but it's hung on unusually concrete data points — a customer-support RCT where novices gained 34% and experts barely moved; Cursor's 2025 default-agent rollout lifting merges 52% at experienced firms vs 23% elsewhere (agents flip the beginner-lift finding); Veracode's 45% OWASP-flaw rate in AI-generated code; and the METR study where experienced devs took 19% longer with AI while believing they were 20% faster. Marketing-adjacent (AE Studio sells dev services in the last paragraph), but the "agents reward the person who can tell a right answer from a wrong one" claim is a real seam for `agents-in-real-deployment` economics.
