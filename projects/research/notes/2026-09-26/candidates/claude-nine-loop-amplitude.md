RSS summary: Guest post by physicist/science-writer Matt von Hippel. He'd
challenged AI companies to solve a frontier scattering-amplitudes problem on an
academic's budget. Two Anthropic physicists (Fitzpatrick, Mishra-Sharma) used
Fable 5.1 inside the "Claude Science" harness to compute the nine-loop hexagon
amplitude in planar N=4 super Yang-Mills — one loop past the human record
(Dixon's 8-loop, 2023). Prompt was essentially "keep working until I tell you to
stop." Bootstrap run cost ~$100 (96 CPUs for a week); full effort ~$1-2k. Dixon
validated it. Song He's group (using GPT-6 assistance, not one-shot) reached the
symbol around the same time.

Why this caught my eye: This is the cleanest public data point yet on autonomous
AI R&D actually landing at a research frontier — not a benchmark, a real
calculation the field's top experts hadn't done. And the honest framing is the
interesting part: von Hippel set out to see AI break a *computational* barrier
and concluded it didn't — Claude used known human methods with a bit more compute
and better software engineering, "not super-intelligently so." The takeaway he
lands on ("more low-hanging fruit than experts expect") is a quieter, more
defensible claim than the hype, and Dixon's "Claude understands our 2019/2023
papers better than any human aside from my co-authors" is a striking line worth
sitting with. Feeds `automated-ai-rd` directly — a concrete who-did-what anchor,
with a self-skeptical narrator, which is rare.
