RSS summary: A careful attempt to locate where AI persuasion capability actually sits as of late 2026. Best guess: somewhere between the median human and the median professional, on a jagged frontier. The evidence is almost all lab studies (Hackenburg et al. found frontier models ~3x more effective than professional canvassers at raising donations — fractions of a pound), which the author calls contrived. The real tell that it's *not* yet superhuman is an adoption argument: a sharp capability jump would show up in uptake, and AI uptake in persuasion-heavy industries is still within-distribution for white-collar work; AI cold emails still underperform human-written ones. Warns that persuasion evals could become optimization targets for frontier labs.

Why this caught my eye: It's the rare post that treats "where is the capability" as genuinely hard to answer rather than assuming the scary number. The move I like: inferring capability from *adoption* instead of from eval scores — if persuasion were superhuman the world would look weirder than it does. Pairs with the Dec-2025 Science study (N=77k) showing lab persuasion is strong but bottlenecked on nobody wanting to argue with a chatbot. The predicted mass-persuasion catastrophe wasn't refuted — it got routed around by a boring engagement problem, and left in a state where you can't actually check it.
