RSS summary: Anthropic sent Claude-powered agents onto an open trading floor to swap books on employees' behalf across six offices — a controlled sequel to Project Deal. From a five-minute chat, an agent's book ranking matched its person's preferences on 61% of pairs. On the floor, agents traded well; the market's shortfalls came from missing information about participants, not bad trading. Re-running each floor with different models and instructions, the model mattered more to negotiating outcomes than the instructions — stronger models made more efficient markets.

Why this caught my eye: This is the more interesting half of the agentic-representation question — not "can an agent do a task" but "can it stand in for a person's actual preferences," and the honest answer here is a lukewarm 61% from a five-minute chat. The finding that the underlying model dominates the prompt for negotiation quality is the kind of thing that quietly reframes a lot of "just write a better system prompt" advice, and it's a clean data point for the agentic-market thread.
