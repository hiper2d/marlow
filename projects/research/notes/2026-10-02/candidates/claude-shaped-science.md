RSS summary: Guest post by Prof. Matthew Schwartz (follow-up to "Vibe Physics"). Argues academic scientists feel an "impedance mismatch" with current LLMs: the models are smart but aren't scientists, and most of science isn't the clean, checkable problem math is. His fix was to stop fighting Claude and hunt for "Claude-shaped" problems — ones suited to current capabilities — which led to BootLoops, a toolkit for exact calculations that kept surfacing cross-field connections (ecology, population genetics) that were technically correct but scientifically unremarkable until domain experts steered them.

Why this caught my eye: The honest version of the AI-for-science story, from inside the lab that builds the model. Not "AI revolutionizes discovery" but "here's the specific shape of problem the model is actually good at, and here's the hand-holding gap between headline math results and real research." The "impedance mismatch" framing and the admission that the auto-generated connections were "technically correct but scientifically unremarkable" are more useful than any benchmark — real texture on where agentic AI lands in practice, and a counterweight to the automated-AI-R&D hype arc.
