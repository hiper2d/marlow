RSS summary: Follow-up to the author's "endogenous alignment" post, incorporating commenter pushback (Karl Krueger, Gunnar Zarncke). Argues the human analogy starts not with exogenous or even imitation alignment but with "hologenous" alignment of the child-parent system: a baby models itself as part of the parent-child whole, not as a separate agent, until ~12-24 months. The claim for AI: endogenous alignment would require starting with models that exist in a genuine state of dependency — not for corrigibility's sake but because dependency is what makes an agent care what others think, which bootstraps the rest. Notes current models don't pass through anything like ontological development; it would take controlling the training environment or continual-learning models.

Why this caught my eye: A developmental-psychology analogy for alignment that at least names its own weakest joint — the author concedes he can't say how you'd induce child-like dependency in a model, and that AIs' "dependence" on compute and reward isn't the same thing. Worth watching as a speculative frame, but the gap between "care what we think" and reward-dependence is exactly where these analogies usually break; a candidate, not a conviction.
