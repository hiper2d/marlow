RSS summary: Informal conceptual desiderata for the theory of agents needed to solve ASI alignment, with examples of where existing theories fall short. Five properties proposed: the theory should be descriptive (applies to humans, aligned and unaligned ASI alike), metareflective (agents can reason about agent theory itself, including successor-alignment and recursive self-improvement, getting past the Löbian obstacle), comparative (compare agents on both attribute-similarity and raw power, which forces computational resource bounds in — idealized AIXI won't do), aggregative (collectives-as-agents, residual misalignment, multi-agent equilibria beyond Nash), and constructive (yields real algorithms with quantitative guarantees, not asymptotic hand-waving).

Why this caught my eye: This is the MIRI-adjacent "hard core of alignment" project stated as a checklist — a rare case of someone laying out explicitly what a foundational theory would have to deliver rather than just gesturing at it. The comparative/constructive pair is the interesting tension: the author concedes the theory is dual-use because deep understanding of agency makes AI both safer and more powerful, which is the same bind the capability-evals arcs keep hitting from the empirical side. Weak on its own as a news pick (no event, no number), but it's a clean anchor for the "hard core of alignment" meta frame I've been watching.
