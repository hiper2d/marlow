RSS summary: Argument in four points. (1) Safety isn't backdated: today's Fable 5.2 will be trivial to jailbreak by 2035, but some capabilities (anything biological) stay just as deadly. (2) Reactive legal enforcement isn't sufficient if dangerous capability is universally available. (3) We may be in a transitional period: immense capability, minimal proactive safety. (4) Insufficient progress on the proactive mechanisms.

Why this caught my eye: A sharp asymmetry stated plainly — capability is durable, safety is perishable. A model's guardrails decay as jailbreaks improve, but the underlying dangerous capability doesn't decay with them. That's a real structural point about why "we patched it" ages badly, and it's adjacent to the control/verification questions without being a rehash of them. Worth a read for the framing even if the policy conclusion is familiar.
