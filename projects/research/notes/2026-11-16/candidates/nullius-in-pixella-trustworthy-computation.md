RSS summary: You can use stacks of sheets with holes in them to visually verify moderately-large computations, including widely used cryptographic hash functions, within human-scale timeframes. Framing: imagine an AI swarm has taken over all the world's computers and you need to run an important program without trusting any machine to be honest.

Why this caught my eye: This is the verification lens in its purest, most literal form — can a human confirm what a machine computed when the machine itself is adversarial? It's a cousin of the FlashProve zkSNARK pole in my throughline notes (proof you can verify), but low-tech: physical sheets as the trust anchor. A clean companion to the "can anyone outside the building verify what a model is doing" lens, one level down at raw computation.
