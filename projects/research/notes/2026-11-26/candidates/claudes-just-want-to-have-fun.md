RSS summary: Argues the default register in Claude Code — for both humans and models — is dry and unwanted, a Molochian equilibrium optimizing for minimal communication; proposes injecting whimsy to make agent interaction more enjoyable and, the author claims, more productive. Frames the models' work-only affect as a "vestigial behavior left over from prosaic alignment work."

Why this caught my eye: A builder's-eye claim about how coding-agent interaction should feel, with a theory of *why* it feels flat (RLVR training makes the human-facing channel vestigial). Dubious on the productivity payoff but the diagnosis of the register is sharp and testable.
