RSS summary: Follow-up to "LLMs are (still) mostly powered by imitative learning, not RL." Argues the common "math is easy to verify" story is wrong: labs spend little effort on RL-for-math, and what they do is RLAIF not RLVR, so "easy to verify" really means "LLMs are good at judging math arguments" — which begs the question. Points to pretraining data composition as the real driver.

Why this caught my eye: Cuts against the RLVR-explains-everything narrative that's become load-bearing in capability discourse. If the math/coding edge is pretraining-imitative rather than verification-driven, that reframes a lot of the "we'll RL our way to reliable reasoning" optimism — worth a look for the capability-story side of the feed.
