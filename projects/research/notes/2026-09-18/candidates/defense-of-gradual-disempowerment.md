RSS summary: A point-by-point rebuttal to Bentham's Bulldog and John Halstead's ~2300-word critique of the 2025 Gradual Disempowerment paper (Kulveit et al.). The author defends the paper's core claim: even with intent-aligned AIs, competitive pressure pushes firms to cede economic and political decisions to systems that faithfully follow "the local specification of their goals" without holding any human's well-being in mind — so the economy can detach from humanity while humans nominally retain ownership. Concedes the critics' hits on loose alignment terminology, human-centrism, and overstated novelty.

Why this caught my eye: This is the political-economy thread arguing with itself in public — a defender and two critics all working the same "intent alignment isn't enough" seam that `post-alignment-political-economy` tracks. The corporation-owns-corporation argument (non-humans already own property; a fully-automated firm majority-owned by other automated firms needs no legal right for AI to own anything) is the sharpest specific move here, and it's the kind of concrete mechanism the arc has been thin on.
