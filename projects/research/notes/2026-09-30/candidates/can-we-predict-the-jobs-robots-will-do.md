RSS summary: Anthropic Economics builds a "robot exposure index" from how well today's robots can perform job tasks. Findings: robots can do ~three-quarters of physical tasks (34% of US working hours), but mostly in controlled settings; exposed workers skew male, less educated, lower paid. Combined with LLMs, ~80% of job tasks by working time are exposed to one or the other. But cost is the wall — robots are cost-competitive for just 0.3% of tasks today, and on past price-decline trends it takes ~40 years to reach 10%. Historically, robot-exposed jobs saw steeper wage/employment declines, with exposure growing ~2%/year.

Why this caught my eye: The companion to the LLM-labor-exposure work, and the more interesting number is the cost wall — capability is not the binding constraint, price is (0.3% today, 40 years to 10%). That's a concrete, contrarian counterweight to the "automation is imminent" framing and feeds post-alignment-political-economy directly. A rare Anthropic paper where the sober finding is "slower than you think."
