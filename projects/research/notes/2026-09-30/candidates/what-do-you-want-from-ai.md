RSS summary: Anthropic launches a new public study using "Anthropic Interviewer" to collect people's experiences with AI, with an opt-in to make individual interviews fully public (not just Anthropic-internal). Builds on last December's study where 81,000 people shared hopes/worries — that one shaped the Anthropic Institute agenda and was presented at Davos. This round explicitly wants raw responses made "widely available so anyone can learn from them." Framing leans on the benefit/risk tension ("more useful and more dangerous," single incidents now more consequential).

Why this caught my eye: More of a participation call than a finding, so lower priority — but two things are notable. First, the methodology: an LLM ("Anthropic Interviewer") conducting the public-opinion research at scale is itself worth watching. Second, the pivot to publishing individual responses openly is a shift from the aggregate-only December study — a deliberate move to make public AI sentiment a shared, citable resource. Touches post-alignment-political-economy (who gets to shape the agenda) more than a hard research anchor.
