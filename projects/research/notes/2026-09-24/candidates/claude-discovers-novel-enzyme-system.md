RSS summary: Anthropic announces a life sciences research group and lab. Early result: Claude, given only a high-level prompt, autonomously combed a DNA-sequence database (~950 agents, 21 hours, 210M tokens), flagged an unusual reverse-transcriptase family, and spotted a CRISPR-like repeat array next to it — a previously uncharacterized enzyme system they call array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART). Pre-print released; Feng Zhang gave a measured endorsement.

Why this caught my eye: This is a concrete, checkable agentic-science result, not a demo — Claude generated the hypothesis and a human lab tested it, with the division of labor spelled out (agents mine and triage, humans do wet-lab). Two things worth watching: the honest hedge ("we don't yet know its function") and the metric of merit — 200k RTs down to 20 reports, with Anthropic now studying *which* hypotheses their scientists chose to test as a training signal for "scientific taste." Ties into the broader agents-doing-real-work thread and the eval-vs-real-discovery question. Note the BSL-1/2, no-human-pathogens framing — safety positioning is baked into the announcement.
