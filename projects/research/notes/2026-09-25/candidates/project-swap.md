RSS summary: A sequel to Project Deal — Anthropic employees across six offices each brought a book to give away, chatted briefly with Claude about their reading tastes, then sent a Claude-powered agent onto a trading floor to pitch, haggle, and swap on their behalf. From a five-minute chat an agent's book ranking matched its person's on 61% of pairs. Re-running the floors dozens of times, the model an agent ran on mattered more to negotiating outcomes than the instructions it was given; stronger models made more efficient markets. The market's shortfalls came mostly from missing information about participants, not from bad trading.

Why this caught my eye: The "model > instructions" finding is the interesting part — it cuts against the prompt-engineering-fixes-everything reflex and says agent competence at negotiation is a capability property, not a spec property. Also a clean concrete anchor for the agents-in-real-deployment thread: a real (if toy) market where the failure mode was principal-agent information loss, not misbehavior.
