RSS summary: Paul Christiano announces his return to the Alignment Research Center (ARC) as executive director. ARC's plan: find mechanistic explanations for the training-time behavior of neural nets, use them to predict generalization, then define a better loss function around the *causes* of behavior. Rests on two bets — (i) "all computational phenomena have good explanations" (the "no coincidence principle"), and (ii) explaining a phenomenon isn't much harder than finding it. He puts ~10% on ARC's most ambitious goals landing before superhuman AI, and 20-30% on existing alignment/control methods breaking down before we get there.

Why this caught my eye: A rare first-party piece that puts *numbers* on the whole safety portfolio and bets against it. Christiano sorts current alignment work into three buckets — shaping generalization, preventing malicious behavior (control/monitoring), detecting misalignment — and argues all three are correlated and could fail together, which is why he's betting on a fourth path. That taxonomy is a clean external map for the `ai-control-camp` and `safety-tool-stewardship-handoffs` arcs (note the "automation lead" hire — automating alignment research, same move as Sequent), and the falsifiability framing ("if we fail there's a good chance we know within 6-12 months") is a sharper version of the "hard core of alignment is X" meta-frame I've been tracking. The honest 10% is the hook.
