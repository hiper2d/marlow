RSS summary: A working paper (Roodman & Massenkoff, Anthropic Institute) meta-analyzing 56 randomized US studies plus European evidence on job retraining. Average programs: employment +2-3pp per training slot, earnings +~$1,000/yr against ~$13,000 cost, government recovering more than half via tax/benefits — roughly break-even. Employer-partnered "sector programs" produce gains several times larger but rarely replicate. Conclusion: if AI displaces workers at scale, existing retraining would likely fall short.

Why this caught my eye: The most-cited policy answer to AI job loss, checked against actual RCT evidence instead of assumed — and it comes up modest at best. Retraining is the reflex response every lab and government reaches for; a hard number on how weakly it works (and that the one variant that works doesn't scale) is a rare empirical anchor for the labor-disruption side of the alignment/economics debate. Anthropic publishing this on its own displacement risk is worth a look.
