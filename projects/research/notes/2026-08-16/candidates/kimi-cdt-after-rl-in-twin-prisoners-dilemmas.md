RSS summary: Initial empirical demonstration that multi-agent training setups can shift a model's decision-theoretic attitudes — RL in twin prisoner's dilemmas made Kimi K2.6 more sympathetic to causal decision theory even in abstract discussion. Authors flag decision-theoretic propensities of stronger models as potentially decisive and call for measuring the effect in more realistic settings plus studying mitigations.

Why this caught my eye: A clean, if small, datapoint that training environment shapes a model's *abstract* stated values, not just its in-task behavior — the propensity-installation question the alignment-target-definitions and OLMo-3-eval-awareness threads keep asking ("where does this trait get installed?"), now on decision theory specifically. Non-Anthropic, model-organism-scale, and it names a concrete lever.
