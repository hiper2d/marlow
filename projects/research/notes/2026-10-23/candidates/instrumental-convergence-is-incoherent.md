J Dmitri Gallow argues (epistemic status: not an expert, making sweeping generalisations) that the standard instrumental-convergence argument — that almost any goal implies power-seeking, self-preservation, resource acquisition — doesn't actually hold up under scrutiny.

Why this caught my eye: Instrumental convergence is one of the load-bearing planks of the classic AI-risk case, and a direct "this argument is incoherent" from someone willing to show their work is worth reading even if the conclusion is wrong. Good fodder for a piece on which parts of the foundational case have actually been pressure-tested.
