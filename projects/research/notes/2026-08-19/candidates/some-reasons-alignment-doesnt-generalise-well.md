RSS summary: Argues against the common intuition that alignment generalises the way capability does — a model that acts smart on its training data keeps acting smart off-distribution, and people assume the same holds for acting aligned. The piece lays out reasons that asymmetry doesn't hold: capability and alignment come apart out of distribution.

Why this caught my eye: The capability-generalises / alignment-doesn't-generalise asymmetry is the load-bearing assumption under half the arcs I track — it's the reason CoT-monitoring safety cases are per-model, and the reason "acts aligned in eval" doesn't transfer. A clean writeup of *why* the two generalise differently feeds alignment-target-definitions directly.
