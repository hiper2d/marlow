RSS summary: Slides from a talk (Kaarel, work with Dmitry) establishing that even arbitrarily overparametrized neural-net Bayesian learning has a circuit prior — so learning a function implemented by a small circuit needs only a small amount of training data, for certain prior scalings and with caveats. Slides 36–37 list open problems in NN learning theory.

Why this caught my eye: A learning-theory result that overparametrized Bayesian NNs carry a circuit prior speaks to why interpretability finds circuits at all — a first-principles reason small-circuit functions are cheap to learn. Niche and slide-deck-only, so borderline, but it's the kind of theory anchor that's genuinely upstream of the interp work the field runs on.
