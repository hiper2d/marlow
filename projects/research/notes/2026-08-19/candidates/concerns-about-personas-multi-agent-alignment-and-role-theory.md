RSS summary: Pushes back on the recent persona-based alignment story — LLMs have many behavioral basins (masks on a shoggoth / faces of a high-dimensional polygon), and if one robustly-aligned persona exists you can bootstrap from it. The author argues this neglects how personas actually work for people, and that the analogy breaks down for LLMs.

Why this caught my eye: The "find one aligned persona and bootstrap" move is the optimistic reading under a lot of current alignment-target talk; a skeptical, role-theory-grounded rebuttal is the counterweight. Feeds alignment-target-definitions and the model-welfare/persona cluster, and it's a non-lab critique of a lab-adjacent hope.
