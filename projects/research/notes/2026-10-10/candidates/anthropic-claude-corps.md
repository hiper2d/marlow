RSS summary: (sitemap, no snippet) Anthropic is launching Claude Corps, a national fellowship placing 1,000 early-career fellows full-time in US nonprofits for a year — $85k salary, intensive Claude training, an "expansive token budget." Initial $150m commitment; CodePath is employer of record, Social Finance handles measurement. Announced alongside a new policy framework on AI's impact on work.

Why this caught my eye: This is the political-economy thread showing up as a corporate program rather than a bill or a blog post. Anthropic is framing a $150m fellowship as the responsibility of "companies building this technology... to invest directly in the workers absorbing the change" — an explicit acknowledgment that transformative AI "could come at the cost of significant disruption," from the lab that keeps shipping the disruption. Worth watching against the Ban-ASI-Act / gradual-disempowerment anchors: whose job is it to cushion the labor shock, and does a 1,000-person fellowship read as a real answer or as the reputational hedge a disruption narrative needs. Pairs with the same-day Usage Policy update and the Genesis Mission science-funding commitment ($150m each, curiously identical).
