RSS summary: A core hope for managing AI risks is that AIs will help us understand the situation, plan for what lies ahead, and develop mitigations. New index aims to measure the conceptual-reasoning ability that this hope leans on — the capability to reason well about novel, underspecified, high-level problems rather than crisp benchmark tasks. Authors plan follow-up posts arguing the case for the work.

Why this caught my eye: This is a measure aimed squarely at the "will AIs actually be able to do the alignment/planning work we're counting on" question — the automated-alignment / safety-tool-stewardship hope stated as something falsifiable. Sits next to the automated-ai-rd proxy debate (LoC-merged, FrontierCode, Claude Tag) but from the *conceptual*-capability side rather than the coding-throughput side, which is exactly the axis those proxies don't touch. Worth a body-fetch to see how they operationalize "conceptual reasoning" — the whole value hinges on whether the index measures something distinct from existing reasoning benchmarks or just relabels them.
