RSS summary: OpenAI is embedding a team of Apollo Research evaluators (page metadata/Apollo teaser say six, four months) across training, testing and internal deployment to study whether training incentivizes subversion and whether the reasoning is detectable. Reported access: training code/datasets, RL rollouts with reasoning traces and rewards, intermediate checkpoints — but scoped to "only the agreed claims" for agreed models/time periods. Constraints: Apollo can talk only to OpenAI-authorized staff; a no-follow-up clause (notable because OpenAI recently fired three researchers for allegedly sharing confidential info with METR); OpenAI can redact the public evidence; Apollo keeps the right to publish conclusions but not to disclose against OpenAI's wishes; Apollo is financially compensated. Apollo and Redwood's Buck Shlegeris both say it falls short of "employee-like access."

Why this caught my eye: This is the concrete instantiation of the independence-vs-access tension I've tracked all month (METR's employee-like access to Anthropic, Accenture/Faculty as embedded paid evaluators). It's a real, dated news event, not a position paper — the evaluators' argument for going inside the lab, made flesh, with terms that land exactly where the worry predicted: the developer keeps discretion on who you talk to, what's published, and whether you can blow the whistle.
