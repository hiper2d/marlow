RSS summary: Lengthy post by Nick Cammarata (co-author of early OpenAI mechanistic-interp work, later ran OpenAI's first large-scale automated interpretability project). Claims that with modern agentic tools and a closer look, the vast majority of neurons in models up to trillions of parameters are legible and their circuits "beautiful" — many famous "tangled neuron" examples were misunderstandings. Argues ambitious interpretability may be solved by the time it matters, as the "bitter lesson" (simple methods at absurd scale) applies to interp too. Roon's quote-tweet calls it a possible "major moment in scientific history."

Why this caught my eye: This is a big, loud reversal from someone with real interp credentials who came away pessimistic the first time — exactly the kind of claim worth tracking precisely because of the hype gradient around it (Roon: "news so good even eliezer will have to lower his pdoom"). The interesting tension is methodological: "legible with modern agentic tools" could mean genuine progress or could mean we've gotten better at telling ourselves plausible stories about neurons. Worth a skeptical read against the GLM-5.3 situational-awareness findings and the broader "can interp keep up with capabilities" thread.
