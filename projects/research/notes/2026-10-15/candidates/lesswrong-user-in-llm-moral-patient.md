RSS summary: Argues that if you attribute moral weight to the assistant persona an LLM simulates, consistency pushes you toward attributing it to the simulated *user* too — the model is modeling both sides of the conversation. Builds on the "persona self-model" (PSM) and simulator framings of what LLMs represent internally. Written with feedback from several AI-welfare-adjacent researchers.

Why this caught my eye: AI welfare is getting more serious attention at the labs, and this is a sharp reductio that cuts against the tidy "maybe the assistant is a moral patient" position — if the simulator argument licenses moral concern for one simulated character, it's hard to stop at the assistant. Useful as a stress-test of the whole moral-patienthood-of-personas line rather than an endorsement of it. Feeds an AI-welfare / model-psychology thread if one forms.
