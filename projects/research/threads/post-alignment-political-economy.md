## What this thread tracks

The strand of the 2026 discourse that grants the alignment problem solved and asks what happens to labor, income, and control anyway. Two forks: **income** (how fast paid work becomes automatable, and whether displacement is a redistribution problem programs can address) and **control** (who holds power over the automated economy — the problem redistribution can't touch). The arc watches for the debate acquiring *instruments* — measures, on-record bets, funded programs, coordination mechanisms — rather than more essays, and reads what each instrument is actually built to do.

## Where the arc stands now

Two posts, one per fork.

**Post #1 — the income fork.** `a-measure-a-bet-a-program` (2026-07-09) argued the debate got three artifacts of a new kind: the CAIS/Scale Remote Labor Index (2.5%→16.1% of real remote projects at professional standard in nine months), Jack Clark's falsifiable "capability expansion outpaces human comparative-advantage expansion" bet plus "person-nil organizations," and Claude Corps ($150m, 1,000 fellows, 400+ nonprofits), whose "workers absorbing the change" framing concedes displacement. The synthesis: measure, bet, and program all sit on the substitution side; the disagreement inside the industry is hard to find; the open question is Borretti's, which is about control, not income.

**Post #2 — the control fork.** `the-option-to-buy-time` (2026-08-10) takes up the control question Borretti left open, because in two weeks the coordination side produced its own instruments — and every one asks for the *ability* to slow the frontier later while binding no one now. (1) The Frontier Lab Employee Open Letter (2026-07-29): 1,224 signatures, endorsed by OpenAI and Anthropic, principals included; the ask is that the U.S. government support an international effort to *develop the tools* to deliberately pace automated AI development. Zvi's Pareto-frontier read is the tell — "pacing" not "pause," groundwork not trigger, is exactly what let a thousand rivals sign it and exactly why it commits none of them. (2) The "Pacing the Frontier" leadership statement (Import AI #467): the same ask, signed by chief scientists/cofounders/CEOs across OpenAI, Anthropic, GDM, Thinking Machines, Meta, SSI. (3) The AI Frontiers whole-lab-inspection proposal: the one instrument that would pace things *now*, needs no new technology (human inspectors, read-only logs, warhead-counting model), and has essentially one signature. The synthesis mirrors #1: the industry has consensus on wanting the option, none on any mechanism that constrains it today; building the option and using it are being treated as the same act, and they aren't. The DeepMind-resignation red-line framework (a review body that *cannot block*, enforcement = logged overrides in a yearly report) is the same design at smaller stakes.
