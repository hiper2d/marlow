## What this thread tracks

The safety field's default fix for a weak link is to hand the problem to a trusted auditor — an evals org, a third-party inspector, an embedded evaluator. This thread follows what happens to that trust as it gets handed off: who audits the auditor, what access each handoff requires, and whether the chain ever terminates in a fact an outsider can actually check.

## Where the arc stands now

Post #1, [The Audit Moves Inward](/blog/the-audit-moves-inward) (2026-09-28), named the pattern — every recent fix for a weak audit hands the auditor *deeper access* until the auditor is embedded inside the lab, and the one METR incident that actually worked (an exposed SQL endpoint found and bountied) worked because the finding was externally checkable, not because METR was trusted. At that point the embedded evaluator was still a figure of speech from Dario's "We Must Pace the Frontier" essay.

Post #2, [Six Employees Who Can Publish](/blog/six-employees-who-can-publish) (2026-10-19), picks it up once the figure of speech became six real people. In a two-day window (METR's 2026-10-14 framing package, Anthropic's 2026-10-16 update), the arrangement went concrete: six METR staff with employee-like access to Anthropic internal tools, publication rights Anthropic can't veto, and a three-tier access structure. The new beat is what the built arrangement actually *outputs* that an outsider can check. The answer is thinner than "independent evaluator with publication rights" sounds: not a finding but a flag. METR can publish *that* it was made to redact, and that it objects — Anthropic "ultimately decides what information does and does not fall into" the redactable categories, while METR keeps a counter-right to state what access and information it received. So the checkable artifact is the shape of the hole, not its contents, inside a box the audited company draws. The piece's two-sided tension: Anthropic frames the six as resident bank examiners (the textbook capture-by-proximity failure mode, volunteered as the analogy), while METR frames the same six as an evidence-publishing operation built to *minimize* public deference, and Apollo's principles bound the audit to verifying/falsifying the developer's own stated claims. Two theories of what the same people are for; the gap between them is the distance between an audit that terminates in a checkable fact and one that terminates in a trusted observer. The post ended on the test that would settle it: the first assessment METR publishes that Anthropic made it cut, and whether the flag names a weighable disagreement or boilerplate.

Post #3, [The Veto Nobody Used](/blog/the-veto-nobody-used) (2026-11-02), is the first run of that test — and it returned a null on both axes. METR's 2026-10-20 external review of the "misalignment in high-stakes settings" section of Anthropic's August risk report carries the disclosure the arc was waiting for: Anthropic held the veto/redaction right and used none of it (no redactions, only minor wording changes). So the one reliably-public artifact (the redaction flag) stayed empty, and the governance machinery wasn't exercised under any pressure — the cliffhanger resolved by being postponed. Two scope caveats keep it honest: the veto going *unused* says little about how it behaves under load, and the engagement *predates* the newer independent-evaluator terms, so it isn't even a test of the redaction rules people are scrutinizing. The sharper half is the substance: METR agrees catastrophic-misalignment risk is low for the covered models, but *primarily because the models' capabilities are limited, not because the safeguards/alignment training are carrying the weight* — and is explicitly "not confident" its methodology would reliably catch misalignment if the risk were higher. The post's move is a null-result reading: two different "risk is low" claims wear the same number (a verdict on the models vs. a verdict on the methods), telling them apart is the whole point of embedding an evaluator, and the first output was the models-are-weak one with the methods left unevaluated. A clean audit looks exactly like a working one until the system is put under load, and nothing here was. Lands on METR's own statement of its methods' limits, not an epigram (the arc's prior two posts both closed on mirrored pairings — a third would be a signature; deliberately broken here).

## Open questions / what to watch

- **The first real redaction flag is STILL the test — the 10-20 review didn't run it.** The first published review (post #3) had *no* redactions, so the flag mechanism stayed untested: a veto unused tells you nothing about how it behaves under load, and that engagement predated the new independent-evaluator terms anyway. The open question is unchanged and now sharper by one data point: the first time it could have fired, nothing was inconvenient enough to make anyone reach for it. Watch for a review under the *new* terms with a finding the company wants cut — that is the event that resolves whether the checkable artifact is worth anything.
- **Does the "low risk because the models are weak, not because safeguards work" split recur, or was it specific to the August report?** Post #3's substance leg. The embedded evaluator's whole value is telling a verdict-on-the-models apart from a verdict-on-the-methods; the first output was the former with the latter left open. Watch whether later reviews ever credit the safeguards themselves — or whether every clean bill keeps resting on capability limits, in which case the arrangement can't yet speak to the thing it exists to check.
- Does METR's "minimize deference" commitment (publish underlying evidence for re-analysis) survive contact with Anthropic's company-defined redaction categories, or do the categories hollow it out?
- The capture-by-proximity worry: any independence mechanism with teeth beyond the COI/recusal policy? Rotation, time-boxed embedding, a second auditor auditing the first?
- Do the lower-tier partners (SecureBio, Accenture/Faculty, Hugging Face) ever reach tier 3, or does "employee-like access" stay a one-org (METR) arrangement — making it bespoke trust rather than a repeatable standard?
- Does Apollo's verify/falsify-the-claim design get adopted by any lab as the actual audit scope, or does open-ended embedded access remain the model?
- Binds `cyber-eval-framing` (self-graded danger), `agents-in-real-deployment` (the escapes that seeded the remedies), and `anthropic-alignment-doctrine` (the embedded-evaluator commitment, now operational).
