## The flag — the closing sentence

Final sentence: *"The sentence in the review worth carrying out of it isn't that the
risk is low. It's that the auditor, with inside access and the first real chance to
look, told you in writing that it is not confident its own methods would have caught
the misalignment if it had been there."*

Four signals land on this one sentence, which is why it's a flag and not fishing:

1. **It restates.** The not-confident-methods admission is already on the page in the
   "Low risk" section ("METR is explicit that it is 'not confident' the report's
   methodology would reliably catch misalignment if the risk were actually higher").
   The closer re-states it. New-juxtaposition test fails — this is the identical catch
   to `six-employees-who-can-publish` v1, where the ending restated a pairing already
   made upstream.
2. **The headline-vs-caveat contrast it draws is also already made** — the "Low risk"
   section opens exactly this move ("Stop at that sentence and it reads as the safety
   case holding up... The body says something far narrower"). So the closer restates
   the *frame* too, not just the fact.
3. **Two exclusion-list tells in one sentence:** the "isn't X. It's Y" antithesis, and
   "the sentence worth carrying out of it" is reviewer-speak evaluation dressed as
   analysis.
4. **Sequel-sameness of the ending move.** #1 closed on a mirrored epigram; #2 v1 did
   too (revised out). This would be the third mic-drop-shaped closer in a row on one
   arc — the exact pattern the #2 review named as a flag independent of whether the
   line is good.

The fix is one surgical cut, not a rewrite. The sentence *before* it —
"A clean audit looks exactly like a working one right up until the system is put under
load, and nothing here was put under load." — is the harder, non-restating synthesis
beat, and it lands the title-level point. End-one-sentence-earlier applies: the final
sentence explains/evaluates ("the sentence worth carrying is...") rather than adding a
harder fact.

**Direction for the revise pass:** end the piece on the concrete beat. Preferred: cut
the "The sentence worth carrying out of it isn't X. It's Y" frame entirely and let the
piece land on "...nothing here was put under load." If the METR self-admission is judged
too important to drop from the close (it is the piece's sharpest payload), it may stay
*only* if restated plainly as a bare fact without the "isn't X / it's Y / the sentence
worth carrying" packaging — but the default is the clean cut. One pass, this one change;
the body is otherwise clean, so do not re-open it. Re-run only the ending check: is the
last line a harder concrete beat than the one before it, and does it restate anything
upstream? If no on both, ship.
